📌 Analyst Commentary

Peloton is not necessarily in an active death spiral today — subscription gross margins have remained resilient at ~68%, and the company has dramatically reduced operating losses through restructuring. However, the business model demonstrates classic death spiral risk: approximately 70% of subscription COGS are fixed (studio infrastructure, instructor contracts, content production, platform depreciation). If paid subscribers decline meaningfully below ~2.5M, fixed cost absorption deteriorates rapidly, threatening the subscription margin that is Peloton's primary path to profitability. The denominator effect is the key risk: fixed costs don't shrink when subscribers leave.

Managerial Accounting Concepts
The theoretical framework underlying the death spiral — essential for understanding platform and subscription business models.
📦

Fixed vs. Variable Costs

Fixed costs (studio rent, instructor salaries, platform infrastructure) remain constant regardless of subscriber count. Variable costs (payment processing, some bandwidth) scale with users. Peloton's ~70% fixed COGS ratio creates high operating leverage — great when growing, dangerous when shrinking.

🧮

Overhead Absorption

Fixed overhead is "absorbed" into unit costs by dividing total fixed costs by the number of units (subscribers). When the denominator (subscribers) falls, each remaining subscriber absorbs a larger share of overhead. This is the mechanical engine of the death spiral.

📐

The Denominator Effect

A critical managerial accounting concept: as volume decreases, fixed cost per unit increases non-linearly. A 20% decline in subscribers doesn't produce a 20% increase in cost/sub — it produces a 25% increase (1/0.8 = 1.25×). This convexity accelerates the spiral.

🏭

Excess Capacity Pressure

Peloton's studios, instructor teams, and production infrastructure were built for a larger subscriber base. Excess capacity means fixed costs are being incurred without corresponding revenue. This creates pressure to either cut capacity (risking content quality) or raise prices (risking further churn).

🌀

The Death Spiral in Services

Unlike manufacturing (where excess inventory is the signal), service/platform businesses experience the death spiral through margin compression and churn. The spiral is particularly acute for subscription models because customers can exit monthly, making the feedback loop faster than in capital-intensive industries.

🛡️

Breaking the Spiral

Escape routes include: (1) Grow the denominator — add subscribers via B2B/corporate wellness deals; (2) Reduce fixed costs — outsource production, cut studios; (3) Increase ARPU — premium tiers, app-only plans; (4) Diversify revenue — licensing content to other platforms. Peloton is pursuing all four.
